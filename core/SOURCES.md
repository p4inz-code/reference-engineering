Curated reference sources organized by Pyramid layer and purpose.
Discipline-specific sources live in each discipline's `sources.md`.

Last updated: 2026-06-22

## How to Use This Document

This document covers sources useful across multiple disciplines.
For field-specific sources, see `disciplines/[your-field]/sources.md`.

Sources are rated by type:
- **FREE** — no cost
- **PAID** — subscription or purchase required
- **FREEMIUM** — free tier available, paid for full access

## Layer 1 — Function & Context Sources

### Video Reference

**YouTube** — FREE
The single best source for real-world function reference. Search behavior,
not appearance. "How [thing] works", "[material] under stress", "[material]
failure", "how [object] is made". Slow-motion channels are particularly
useful for behavior reference.

Key channels for function reference:
- The Slow Mo Guys — material behavior at 1000–100,000fps
- Smarter Every Day — mechanical function explanation with close-up footage
- How It's Made — manufacturing process reference (material layer)
- Mark Rober — mechanical problem solving with clear visual explanation

**Vimeo** — FREE / PAID
Higher production quality than YouTube for professional film reference.
Search for documentary footage and behind-the-scenes material.

**Getty Images / Shutterstock** — PAID (preview FREE)
Licensed footage and photography. Preview quality sufficient for reference
extraction even without purchase.

### Technical Documentation

**Wikipedia / Wikimedia Commons** — FREE
Underused by creative practitioners. Technical articles include dimension
specs, material properties, and function descriptions. Diagrams and
schematics for mechanical objects.

**Patent databases (Google Patents, Espacenet)** — FREE
Exploded diagrams and technical specifications for manufactured objects.
Excellent for Layer 1 (function) and Layer 6 (construction method).

**Manufacturer technical documents** — FREE (usually)
Most manufacturers publish PDFs with dimension specs, material specs,
and assembly diagrams. Search "[product name] technical specifications PDF"
or "[product name] datasheet".

## Layer 2 — Scale & Proportion Sources

**Dimensions.com** — FREE
Human-centered scale reference for architecture, furniture, vehicles,
and everyday objects. Every entry includes exact dimensions and a
human figure for scale comparison. Essential Layer 2 resource.

**3D Warehouse (Sketchup)** — FREE
User-uploaded 3D models often include real-world dimensions. Good for
checking scale of architectural and furniture reference.

**Blueprints.com / The-Blueprints.com** — FREE / PAID
Orthographic line drawings for vehicles, aircraft, weapons, and machinery.
Essential for Layer 2 (proportion) and Layer 3 (silhouette stage).

**Aircraft Spruce / Pilot's Handbook** — FREE
Aviation technical documents for precise aircraft reference. Useful for
any project involving aircraft or aerospace design language.

## Layer 3 — Production Stage Sources

### Silhouette / Orthographic Reference

**ArtStation** — FREE / PAID (Pro)
Filter by "2D", "character design", "concept art". Turnaround sheets and
orthographic design sheets for characters and props. Search by genre
(sci-fi, fantasy, realistic) and asset type.

**The-Blueprints.com** — FREE / PAID
Orthographic reference for vehicles, weapons, and machinery. See Layer 2.

**Pinterest** — FREE
Use for silhouette shape language gathering. Set up boards by asset type.
Strong for stylized character and prop silhouette reference.

### Stage-Appropriate Queries

When searching for stage-appropriate reference, modify your search:
- **Silhouette stage:** "[subject] silhouette", "[subject] side view", "[subject] orthographic"
- **Blockout stage:** "[subject] basic shapes", "[subject] form study", "[subject] blockout"
- **Texture stage:** "[material] close up texture", "[surface] macro photography"
- **Hero detail stage:** "[subject] detail photography", "[material] micro", "[subject] close up 4K"

## Layer 4 — Style Rules Sources

**Art station Challenges** — FREE
Competitive art prompts with style constraints. Good for studying how
multiple artists interpret the same style brief — reveals what the style
rules *actually* are vs. what practitioners think they are.

**Concept Art House / Naughty Dog / Santa Monica Studio Art Books** — PAID
Published art books from game studios contain style guides and design
philosophy. Essential for extracting written style rules from professional
productions.

**GDC Vault** — FREE / PAID
Game Developers Conference talks often include visual style development
discussions with explicit rule extraction. Search by game title or
"visual development", "art direction", "style guide".

**ILM / Weta / Framestore "The Art of" books** — PAID
Behind-the-scenes art direction books for film productions. Contain
visual style decision documentation rarely available elsewhere.

## Layer 5 — Lighting Context Sources

**HDRI Haven (Polyhaven)** — FREE
Real-world HDRI environments with known lighting conditions. Use to
understand how materials read under specific real-world lighting.
Labeled by conditions (studio, outdoor overcast, outdoor sunny, etc.)

**Lightroom / Capture One community presets** — FREE / PAID
Photographer-shared presets with labeled lighting styles. Source of
tonal range reference for specific lighting aesthetics.

**Cinematography databases (IMDb Pro, Shot Café)** — PAID
Cinematography-level lighting reference from film productions. Useful
for understanding how controlled lighting builds a specific look.

**ShotDeck** — PAID
Searchable film still database with filtering by color, mood, and
lighting condition. Strong Layer 5 resource for any visual discipline.

## Layer 6 — Material & Surface Sources

**Quixel Megascans** — FREE (with Unreal Engine)
Photogrammetry-scanned real-world surface reference. Best-in-class for
understanding real material behavior in PBR. Even if not using the assets,
use the preview images as material reference.

**TextureHaven (Polyhaven)** — FREE
CC0 PBR materials with documented real-world equivalents. Good for
understanding how real materials translate to PBR channel values.

**Matweb / MatBase** — FREE
Material science databases with physical properties for manufactured
materials. Density, hardness, thermal properties, surface behavior.
Technical Layer 6 reference.

**YouTube: "How It's Made"** — FREE
Manufacturing process reference. Shows construction method, material
behavior under stress, and assembly logic. Layer 6 (material construction).

## Layer 7 — Hero Detail Sources

**Artstation Marketplace** — FREE / PAID
High-resolution reference packs and photogrammetry assets. Free assets
available. Good for hero detail reference on common materials and surfaces.

**Unsplash / Pexels** — FREE
High-resolution photography, CC0 licensed. Good for macro and close-up
surface photography. Search by material type.

**500px / Flickr (macro tags)** — FREE / FREEMIUM
Photographer communities with high-resolution macro photography.
Search "[material] macro" or "[surface] detail".

**Morguefile / Stocksnap** — FREE
CC0 photography archives. Less curated than Unsplash but larger catalog.

## Cross-Discipline Tools

### Reference Organization

**PureRef** — FREE
Dedicated reference board application for visual disciplines. Windows/Mac/Linux.
Non-destructive, lightweight, supports large boards. Cross-promoted with
Kanvaz (github.com/p4inz-code/kanvaz).

**Kanvaz** — FREE (MIT)
Infinite canvas offline reference board by Northbyte Studios. Optimized
for VFX/3D pre-production workflows.
Download: github.com/p4inz-code/kanvaz

**Milanote** — FREE / PAID
Browser-based visual board with text annotation. Good for
annotated reference boards. Collaboration features on paid tier.

**Miro** — FREE / PAID
Collaborative whiteboard. Use for reference boards in team contexts.

### Search Enhancement

**Google Images — Search Operators**
- `filetype:jpg site:artstation.com [subject]` — ArtStation only
- `[subject] -pinterest` — exclude Pinterest results
- `[subject] orthographic` — forces flat/side view results

**TinEye / Google Lens** — FREE
Reverse image search. Find higher-resolution versions of reference
images or locate the original source.

**Behance** — FREE
Adobe's portfolio platform. Strong for UI/UX, brand, and graphic
design reference. Less useful for 3D and VFX.
